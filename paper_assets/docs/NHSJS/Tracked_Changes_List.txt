p5   Delete             Methodology Overview We propose a two-stage sequential filtering framework in which news articles and financial reports are evaluated separately. In Stage One the model analyzes historical news to identify candidate opportunities; in Stage Two financial reports are used to validate and refine those candidates into a final portfolio. This decomposition constrains the model to a well-defined reasoning task at each stage, letting us directly evaluate the impact of structural organization of information on investment performance. The evaluation enforces strict temporal constraints throughout to eliminate the look-ahead and data-snooping biases identified by Li et al. (2025) and others. All input data are sourced from dates strictly preceding each evaluation window, and model-specific training-cutoff restrictions further reduce lookahead risk, ensuring reported performance reflects genuine analytical capability rather than latent knowledge from pre-training.

p7   Insert             News articles and company financial reports are both obtained through the Alpha Vantage application programming interface, news through its news-and-sentiment endpoint and fundamentals through its company-report endpoints, and cached locally. For each decision date, news is drawn from a fixed 24-month window ending at that date; article publication timestamps are parsed to Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) and compared date-only against the window, and duplicate or syndicated stories are removed by exact headline match (first occurrence kept). Because every document is filtered to dates strictly preceding the decision date, no post-decision information can enter a run
p8   Insert             In a representative screening run, the relevance filter retained 110 of 1,382 collected articles (8.0%) under strict investment-relevance criteria. To check the screen against human judgment, we blind-labeled a stratified sample of 180 of these articles (70 screen-passed and 110 screen-rejected) without access to the screen's decisions. The screen achieved a precision of 100% (95% Wilson confidence interval 94.8–100%): every article it passed was independently judged relevant, so the downstream stages are not contaminated by off-topic news. It is, however, deliberately conservative — re-weighting to the 8.0% population pass rate gives an estimated recall of 16% and a false-omission rate of 45% — indicating a high relevance bar rather than exhaustive retrieval; because the pipeline requires a focused, high-precision input set rather than complete coverage, precision is the more consequential property for downstream quality, and we note the lower recall as a limitation. Human–screen agreement was positive but modest (Cohen's kappa = 0.17), a value held down by the low base rate of passed articles, for which kappa is known to understate agreement. The screen is applied by the model without human adjudication during normal operation.

Ablation of Pipeline Components To separate the contribution of sequential structure from context length, and to identify which stages carry the effect, we ran a controlled ablation on GPT-5.1's headline configuration (20→10) across all nine evaluation windows, with three independent generations per condition. Holding the candidate documents fixed, we compared the full two-stage pipeline against five variants that each remove one element: a single prompt over the full candidate pool; a budget-matched single prompt over the identical shortlist documents; a variant that shows the reports but eliminates no candidates; a variant that selects the final portfolio in one step without a staged cut; and a variant with the financial reports shuffled across tickers. The full pipeline achieved the highest mean ROI (52.2%) and outperformed every variant in seven to nine of the nine windows. The single-pass arm carrying the most context underperformed the pipeline by 3.5 percentage points, and the budget-matched single-pass arm — identical documents, one prompt — lost by 4.7 points (sign-test p = 0.039; Wilcoxon p = 0.012), ruling out context length as the driver. Shuffling the reports produced the largest drop (6.5 points; p = 0.012) and the highest run-to-run variance, indicating that the financial-report stage contributes genuine signal rather than formatting. Removing the elimination step or the staged cut each cost roughly 4.6 to 5.1 points. Per-condition token counts and full paired statistics are reported in the Supplementary Information. Because the nine windows overlap and the sample is modest, we read the individual contrasts as directionally consistent — two reach conventional significance and the remainder sit at p ≈ 0.06–0.10 — and emphasize the joint pattern, the full pipeline dominating five independent ablations, as the principal evidence that the effect is structural rather than an artifact of input length.
